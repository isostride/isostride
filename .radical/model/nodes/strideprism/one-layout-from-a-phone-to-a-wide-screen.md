label: "One layout from a phone to a wide screen"
alternatives: |
  1. A separate mobile app or a second set of mobile components: two things to keep in step, and script over the budget.
  2. A hamburger menu that slides the side menu in: one more tap for every change of screen; a bottom tab bar is the pattern of current phone apps for 3–5 destinations.
  3. Tables turned into cards on a phone: readable, but every table needs its own card design; scrolling inside the card keeps one table for both.
  4. A component library with responsive primitives: a dependency on the budget and a second styling system beside the tokens (refused in "Look and feel").
consequences: "A screen added later is responsive by using the same classes; a wide element must scroll inside its card, which the end-to-end test on an emulated phone checks for every screen, tab and view. A page does not fade in: a filling animation keeps the page a layer of its own, so its sheets fall under the bars, and while any fade runs the text is too faint (the accessibility scan fails on it). The blur of the floating side menu is not carried to the phone: a filter on the menu would make it the frame of the fixed tab bar inside it, so on a phone the blur sits on the bar's own layer and the tab bar is nearly opaque. Text on the gradient takes the --on-accent token, which the second hue is chosen to keep readable in both themes. Pinch zoom on the run chart and gestures on the map are not part of this: the chart on a phone is read and selected by lap, drag and the overview."
context: "The runner asked for a UI that is \"super nowoczesny zgodny z trendami\" and responsive on mobile devices, after the first pass for one that is \"bardziej nowoczesny\", and after the second: \"jeszcze odwazniejszy ui\". At phone width the side menu took 236 of 390 px and every screen ran off its edge (Runs by 683 px). The look is one stylesheet of tokens (ADR \"Look and feel\"), every colour a token, each route within 200 KB of script; the app runs in Chromium browsers, on phones in Chrome for Android."
date: "2026-11-21"
decision: "\"One document and one stylesheet for every width: the phone layout is a last section of app/globals.css under a max-width of 820 px, and a section for touch screens (pointer: coarse); no second set of components. The App Shell keeps its elements; the stylesheet lays them out on a wide screen as a side menu that floats off the edges of the window as a deep blue rail in both themes (never black: \"moze byc ciemno blekitny itp ale nie czarny\") (the rail redefines the surface, text and line tokens for itself, so its parts keep their rules), and on a phone as a sticky top bar (logo, search, a menu of the display options) and a tab bar of the screens that floats as an island over the bottom of the page, clear of the notch and the home indicator (viewport-fit=cover with safe-area insets). Rows of choices, tabs and the runs filter scroll in one line; a calendar week is a grid of seven days; wide tables scroll inside their card (a region that takes the focus where it holds no control); sheets, the palette and the chart menu take the screen. The look is carried by the same tokens, bolder in each pass: a gradient of the accent and a second hue (--grad) on the primary button, the screen and the tab in view, the current week and today; the heading of a screen in large heavy type running from ink into the brand's hues (--grad-text, drawn through the letters while their colour stays the ink, so readers and forced colours keep plain text); capsule buttons; rounder cards with a deeper soft shadow; the weeks at a glance as a bento of tiles laid out by the stylesheet alone; figures in tiles; trust labels tinted by status; a two-colour wash behind the page over a grid of dots; fluid type. The colours of the page given to the browser's own chrome (theme-color) mirror the --bg tokens and a test keeps them equal.\""
status: "accepted"
