Everything on one screen, across its whole width (no scrolling at 1440 × 900 and larger). Head: title (or date), date and time, then one compact line of figures (distance, moving time, average pace, average heart rate, elevation gain), device, laps, temperature, "Add to Analysis". Over the chart a strip of laps, one horizontal bar each, a button as wide as the lap on the x axis, named "Lap N · X km" when wide enough: a click selects the lap and zooms to it. Below the head, one card: a toolbar in two rows: the metric pills to choose (up to 4 on, each with its colour swatch); then a short line of what the chart is set to (Bands or Overlaid, by time or distance, blocks drawn) and, at the right end, the "Chart" button (the range shown is announced to screen readers only); the mouse wheel over the chart zooms around the pointer. The "Chart" button slides a menu in from the right, over the map and laps and without dimming the chart: Lines "Bands / Overlaid", Line "Smooth / Raw" (the rolling mean over 1/30 of the range shown, or the 10 s windows), X axis "Time / Distance", Blocks in this run ("Draw on the chart", the list: pointing at one draws it, a click selects it), Make a block (the block types for the selection, or a hint to select first, and "Block from these laps"), Zones (which metrics shown have zones, a link to Settings); it closes with ✕, Escape or the button; the selection is the range shown: a drag on the chart, the wheel, the map, the overview, a lap's bar, a double click in a lap, a laps table row or a block sets it exactly, and the chart and the map show it; under it, side by side, the chart on the left and on its right the route map in one quiet frame (the wheel zooms around the pointer, a drag pans, and the chart then shows the stretch of the route in view; click two points to select; the hint and the OpenStreetMap credit as small overlays in its lower corners, a Fit route button once it was moved by hand) and under it the laps: a head "Laps · N" and a light table, each row with a bar of its pace from the slowest lap to the fastest, the lap shown marked with the accent, scrolled inside (a click on a lap shows it). The chart: the chosen metrics laid over each other, each line scaled to its own range, the focused metric's ticks at the left, laps as alternating bands, kept blocks as washed bands with names only when the toggle "Blocks" in the toolbar is on (off by default) or for the one block pointed at in the list, zones as stripes, the selection as an accent wash, a crosshair with a tooltip listing every line's value and zone at the pointer; two x axes under it, the chosen one (clock or km) and the other at the same places; the focused metric's average as a dashed line and its min and max as points with their values (from the statistics; a Min, Avg or Max cell pointed at lights its mark); a legend of the lines, each with its range, a click focuses one; an overview strip of the whole run whose window is the range shown, growing and shrinking with the zoom, the selection marked under it: dragging the window moves the range and the selection with it, dragging elsewhere selects a new stretch; the chart takes the height the screen leaves. Drag on the chart to select a stretch; pinch or Ctrl/⌘ + scroll zooms around the pointer, a sideways scroll moves. At the bottom, across the full width: the statistics of the selection, the range shown (or of the whole run): its time and distance, the selection told once in its head ("Selection", its clock range, time and distance) and its ✕, which clears it and shows the whole run, a Pa:HR chip (the decoupling of pace from heart rate) for a selection; on the left the figures of the range itself (time, moving time, distance, elevation up and down, the laps covered, the share of the run); on the right a table with a row per metric shown and the columns Min, Avg, Max, for a selection Trend /10 min, and Zones: a bar of the metric's time in zones Z1…Zn in %. The whole view (the range shown, the metrics in their slots, the scale shown, the lines, the x axis, the smoothing, the blocks drawn, the map moved by hand) is kept in the link (from, to, m, f, l, x, s, b, map) and remembered for each run.
